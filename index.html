Fuel-Efficient Vehicles

The Fuel-Smart America Act (H.R. 2035)

Written by Keshu Yang · August 2026

For this project, I wrote a bill that would make fuel-efficient vehicles more affordable without requiring everyone to buy the same kind of car. My proposal combines rebates, gradual efficiency standards, exemptions for specialized vehicles, and public reporting.

Project overview

Why I chose this issue

I chose fuel-efficient vehicles because transportation policy affects both household costs and national energy use. I wanted to write a proposal that improves efficiency but still gives drivers a choice.

  1. 1
    Help buyers at the dealership.
    The bill offers a rebate for qualifying new and used vehicles, with extra help for lower-income buyers.
  2. 2
    Set gradual goals.
    Manufacturers would improve fleet fuel economy over time instead of facing one sudden requirement.
  3. 3
    Keep consumer choice.
    A gasoline, hybrid, electric, or other vehicle can qualify if it meets the same rules.
  4. 4
    Include exemptions and oversight.
    Specialized vehicles are protected, and agencies must publish the program’s costs and results.
